Predicting NBA Scoring from Player Metrics

Regression project

12,844player-seasons, 1996–2023
10predictors in the final model
R² = 0.91variance explained
p < .001every retained predictor

The question

What actually explains an NBA player's scoring output: physical traits like height and weight, or how a player is used within their team's offense? I wanted a model that could isolate the independent effect of each factor once the others were held constant.

Method

Working from a Kaggle dataset of 12,844 player-season observations, I built and evaluated a multiple linear regression model with twelve candidate predictors: physical measures (age, height, weight) and performance metrics (rebounds, assists, usage rate, true shooting percentage, and more).

  • Explored the data with a scatterplot matrix to check for collinearity between predictors
  • Fit a full model, then used backward elimination to drop age and weight, which carried no independent explanatory power
  • Diagnosed non-constant variance and non-normal residuals, then applied a Box-Cox power transformation (λ ≈ 0.42) to correct them
  • Checked variance inflation factors to confirm the remaining predictors weren't dangerously collinear

Findings

Role beats physicalityUsage rate, assists, and rebounds carried the strongest positive effects on scoring. Player weight wasn't significant at all once other variables were controlled for, and height's effect, while significant, was small.

Four coefficients came out with counterintuitive negative signs, including offensive and defensive rebound percentage. That wasn't a mistake: those percentage measures share information with the raw rebound and assist counts already in the model, so once role is accounted for, the percentages mostly reflect positional differences rather than a true negative effect on scoring.

The Effect of Music on Concentration

Randomized experiment

73participants analyzed
9blocking groups
+1.17pt effect, music vs. no music
p = .344not statistically significant

The question

Does listening to music change short-term attention performance, compared to sitting quietly? A lot of people listen to music while studying or working without knowing whether it actually helps.

Method

We designed a block-randomized experiment: participants were grouped by community and, within each group, randomly assigned to either listen to their favorite genre of music for 10 minutes or sit without music. Everyone took a timed attention test before and after, and the outcome was the change in score.

  • Block-randomized protocol designed and piloted by a five-person research team
  • 13 usable observations collected across the music and no-music conditions
  • Full report drafted, from the title and abstract through the scientific question and rationale
  • Treatment effect analyzed with a linear model adjusting for community as a blocking factor

Findings

A null result: reported honestlyThe music group's attention score improved by 1.17 points more than the control group on average (95% CI: −1.28 to 3.62), but the difference was not statistically significant (p = 0.344).

Rather than reframe the analysis to chase significance, we reported the null result as it was and discussed its limitations: a 10-minute window, self-selected music genre, and a volunteer sample that limits how far the conclusion generalizes.

Sustainability at a Private Golf Club

Independent research project

200,000gal/day of reclaimed water required to build
~70%irrigation from reclaimed water
500kWsolar array installed
10+peer-reviewed sources synthesized

The question

How does a private golf club balance genuine environmental investment against symbolic greenwashing, while operating in a region shaped by water scarcity and high member expectations?

Method

I used La Cañada Flintridge Country Club as a case study, combining site visits with an in-depth interview (the club's director of sustainability) and a review of peer-reviewed literature on reclaimed-water irrigation, turf salinity, and golf-course biodiversity, to weigh what the club does against what it claims.

  • Traced the club's history back to its founding condition: it could only be built if it found a use for 200,000 gallons a day of reclaimed water
  • Compared the club's water, solar, and habitat programs against the academic literature on golf-course sustainability
  • Pressure-tested the club's own public claims against what its own staff could and couldn't verify

Findings

Substance behind the imageThe club's most substantiated work, reclaimed water irrigation and native habitat restoration, amounts to a genuinely significant environmental investment, covering nearly three-quarters of its irrigation needs without drawing down local groundwater. Its 500kW solar array builds on that momentum, putting the club among a small group of private golf facilities investing directly in on-site renewable generation.
